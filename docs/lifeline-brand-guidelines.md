# LIFELINE

## Brand Identity & Visual Design Guidelines

**Document type:** Brand standards\
**Version:** 1.0\
**Status:** Proposed foundation for product design\
**Product:** Healthcare referral and emergency patient-transfer
coordination platform\
**Primary market:** Kenya, with a visual system designed to scale across
markets

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Brand foundation

### Brand idea

**Lifeline connects the people, facilities and resources that make a
safe patient transfer possible.**

Lifeline is a healthcare coordination platform that helps referring
facilities, receiving hospitals, ambulance teams and operations
personnel work from a shared, timely view of a patient transfer.

The brand should communicate dependable coordination---not panic,
clinical authority it does not possess, or the appearance of an
ambulance-booking application.

### Brand promise

**Connected care. Coordinated transfers.**

This is a concise expression of the product's intended value. It should
be treated as a brand promise, not a guarantee of a particular clinical
outcome or response time.

### Mission

To make patient referrals and transfers more coordinated, visible and
accountable by connecting healthcare facilities, transport teams and the
information they need to act.

### Vision

A healthcare network in which every participating facility can
coordinate patient referrals and transfers with greater clarity,
confidence and continuity.

### Brand positioning

Lifeline is a referral and patient-transfer coordination platform for
healthcare networks. It supports the operational journey from referral
initiation and receiving-facility coordination through ambulance
dispatch, transfer tracking and handover.

It complements existing hospital information systems, emergency access
channels, ambulance providers and clinical workflows. It does not
replace clinical judgement, emergency services or a hospital's authority
to accept a patient.

### Brand values

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Value                               What it means in the product
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Trust                               Clear information, reliable
                                      workflows, strong privacy and
                                      accountability

  Coordination                        The right stakeholders can see and
                                      act on the relevant transfer
                                      information

  Clarity                             Interfaces reduce ambiguity and
                                      make the next action obvious

  Responsiveness                      Important changes, requests and
                                      escalations are surfaced promptly

  Patient dignity                     Patient information is handled
                                      respectfully and only shared when
                                      appropriate

  Accountability                      Decisions, status changes and
                                      handovers are traceable
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

### Brand personality

-   **Calm:** composed, never alarmist.
-   **Dependable:** consistent, precise and professional.
-   **Human:** respectful of patients and the people caring for them.
-   **Capable:** modern and technically credible without feeling
    complicated.
-   **Connected:** collaborative across facilities and transport teams.

### Brand voice

Use plain, direct and respectful language. Prefer operational clarity
over marketing language.

**Write like this** - "Referral submitted." - "Awaiting
receiving-facility response." - "Ambulance assigned." - "Patient
collected." - "Handover completed." - "Capacity information last updated
at 14:32."

**Avoid** - "We guarantee the fastest emergency response." - "Your
patient is completely safe." - "AI has decided the patient's
priority." - "A bed is guaranteed."

Do not make clinical, availability or response-time promises unless they
are supported by validated operational evidence.

## 2. Brand architecture and naming

### Product name

**Lifeline**

Use the name as one word, with an initial capital in normal copy. Do not
write "Life Line", "LifeLine" or "LIFELINE" in body text. All-uppercase
use is reserved for compact logo treatments or technical labels.

### Descriptor

Use this descriptor where the product's purpose needs clarification:

**Healthcare Referral & Transfer Coordination**

Shorter alternatives for constrained spaces: - Referral & Transfer
Coordination - Patient Transfer Coordination

### Tagline

**Connected care. Coordinated transfers.**

Supporting message: "Coordinate referrals, receiving facilities and
ambulance transfers through one connected workflow."

### Naming rules for modules

Use clear, task-oriented names. The following are proposed module
labels, not necessarily separate products:

-   Referrals
-   Facility Network
-   Receiving Requests
-   Dispatch
-   Ambulances
-   Live Transfers
-   Handover
-   Reports
-   Administration

Avoid overly clever names for clinical or operational functions. Users
should understand what a module does without needing a glossary.

## 3. Visual identity

### Overall direction

Lifeline should look like a trusted healthcare operations platform:
clean, structured, calm and highly legible.

The visual system should combine: - Deep blue for trust, stability and
navigation. - Teal for connection, progress and positive action. -
Light, neutral surfaces for readability. - Carefully controlled status
colours for operational urgency.

Avoid the generic "bright hospital blue everywhere" look, excessive
gradients, decorative medical crosses, heartbeat-line clichés and
unnecessary animation.

### Logo concept

The recommended logo direction is a **connected pathway / care-link
symbol** paired with a confident wordmark.

The symbol may combine: - Two or more connected points representing
referring and receiving facilities. - A continuous path representing the
transfer journey. - A subtle central link or protective form
representing continuity of care.

The mark should remain recognisable at small sizes and work in a single
colour. Do not make an ambulance silhouette the primary logo: Lifeline
coordinates the broader referral journey, not only vehicles.

**Logo lockups** 1. Primary horizontal: symbol + Lifeline wordmark. 2.
Stacked: symbol above wordmark. 3. Symbol-only: app icon, favicon and
compact navigation. 4. Monochrome: single-colour use on constrained
backgrounds.

**Clear space:** Maintain clear space around the logo equal to at least
the height of the symbol's central element.

**Minimum digital size:** Do not display the full horizontal logo below
approximately 120 px wide. Use the symbol-only version in smaller
spaces.

**Backgrounds:** Prefer white or the deep navy brand surface. Ensure
strong contrast. Do not place the logo over busy photography.

The logo concept is a design direction. A final vector logo should be
drawn and approved separately before production use.

### Colour usage proportions

As a general starting point: - 60% white and neutral surfaces. - 25%
navy and dark text/navigation. - 10% teal accents and primary actions. -
5% status colours and other emphasis.

These are visual balance guidelines, not rigid design requirements.

### Accessibility

-   Use WCAG 2.2 AA contrast as the minimum target for text and
    interactive controls.
-   Use dark navy or ink text on light surfaces.
-   Use white text on navy and sufficiently dark teal surfaces.
-   Do not assume the standard teal is suitable for small white text;
    validate contrast before use.
-   Test focus indicators, disabled states, charts, maps and status
    badges independently.
-   Support keyboard navigation and visible focus states.

## 5. Typography

### Recommended typefaces

**Primary: Inter**

Use Inter for interface text, forms, tables, navigation, buttons and
body copy. It is highly legible and works well in dense operational
interfaces.

**Display alternative: Manrope**

Manrope may be used for prominent marketing headings and selected
dashboard titles. Keep the product interface primarily in Inter to
maintain consistency.

If the team wants one font only, use Inter throughout.

### Type scale

  Style               Size     Weight   Line height Typical use
  ------------ ----------- ---------- ------------- -------------------------------
  Display        40--48 px        700          1.15 Marketing hero heading
  H1                 32 px        700           1.2 Main page heading
  H2                 24 px   650--700          1.25 Section heading
  H3                 20 px        600           1.3 Card or subsection heading
  H4                 16 px        600           1.4 Panel heading
  Body large         16 px        400           1.6 Introductory text
  Body           14--15 px        400           1.5 Standard interface text
  Label              13 px   500--600           1.4 Form labels and table headers
  Caption            12 px   400--500           1.4 Metadata and helper text

Do not use very small text for essential clinical or operational
information. Dense tables may use 13--14 px, but should remain readable
at common laptop and tablet sizes.

### Typography rules

-   Use sentence case for page titles, buttons and navigation.
-   Use bold sparingly to establish hierarchy.
-   Avoid long passages in all caps.
-   Use tabular numerals for timestamps, IDs and measurements where
    supported.
-   Use consistent date, time and measurement formatting across the
    application.
-   Do not use font weight alone to distinguish urgent clinical
    information; use labels and structured presentation.

### Font loading

``` css
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Inter:wght@400;500;600;700&family=Manrope:wght@500;600;700;800&display=swap');

:root {
  --font-sans: 'Inter', sans-serif;
  --font-display: 'Manrope', 'Inter', sans-serif;
}
```

For production deployments, consider self-hosting approved font files to
improve control over availability, privacy and performance.

## 6. Layout and interface principles

### Design principles

1.  **Make the next action obvious.** Every role-based screen should
    make the user's next appropriate action easy to identify.
2.  **Show the state of the transfer.** Users should be able to
    distinguish submitted, awaiting response, accepted, assigned,
    collected, in transit, arrived and handed over.
3.  **Separate clinical information from operational status.** They are
    related but not interchangeable.
4.  **Show when information was updated.** Capacity and availability
    information should include a timestamp and, where relevant, its
    source.
5.  **Keep critical information scannable.** Use clear headings,
    structured fields and concise summaries.
6.  **Design for real working conditions.** Support tablets, smaller
    screens, intermittent connectivity and busy clinical environments.
7.  **Protect patient information by default.** Only show information
    required for the user's role and current task.

### Spacing system

Use a 4 px base spacing scale.

  Token          Value Typical use
  ------------ ------- ----------------------------
  `space-1`       4 px Tight icon/text gap
  `space-2`       8 px Compact control spacing
  `space-3`      12 px Form field internals
  `space-4`      16 px Standard component spacing
  `space-5`      20 px Card internals
  `space-6`      24 px Section spacing
  `space-8`      32 px Large section spacing
  `space-10`     40 px Page-level separation
  `space-12`     48 px Major layout separation

### Radius and elevation

  Token              Value Use
  --------------- -------- -------------------------------
  Small radius        6 px Inputs, compact controls
  Medium radius      10 px Cards, menus and panels
  Large radius       14 px Larger feature panels
  Pill              999 px Status chips and compact tags

Use subtle borders as the default separation method. Reserve shadows for
floating menus, dialogs and elevated elements. Avoid heavy shadows
around every card.

### Grid and page layout

-   Use a consistent responsive grid.
-   Keep dashboard content aligned to a shared left edge.
-   Use a persistent, role-appropriate navigation structure on desktop.
-   Collapse navigation thoughtfully on tablet and mobile.
-   Avoid horizontal scrolling for primary workflows.
-   Tables should have clear column labels, sensible density and
    responsive alternatives.

## 7. Core product UI patterns

### Dashboard

A dashboard should prioritise actionable operational information: -
Referrals requiring attention. - Requests awaiting a receiving-facility
response. - Transfers in progress. - Ambulances and crews currently
assigned or available. - Recent activity and escalations.

Avoid filling the dashboard with decorative charts that do not support a
decision.

### Referral status

Use a consistent status vocabulary across the application. Suggested
lifecycle:

1.  Draft
2.  Submitted
3.  Awaiting receiving-facility response
4.  Accepted / Declined / More information required
5.  Awaiting transport assignment
6.  Ambulance assigned
7.  Patient collected
8.  In transit
9.  Arrived
10. Handover completed
11. Cancelled / Closed

Not every referral will pass through every state. Define valid
transitions in the backend and record who made each change and when.

### Status chips

-   Use short, explicit labels.
-   Pair colour with text.
-   Keep chip shape and sizing consistent.
-   Use critical red only for urgent or failed states.
-   Do not make a status appear successful when it is merely pending.

### Forms

-   Use visible labels rather than placeholder-only fields.
-   Explain why sensitive information is requested when appropriate.
-   Group fields by task and clinical/operational purpose.
-   Validate fields inline and provide actionable error messages.
-   Preserve entered information when safe to do so.
-   Distinguish required from optional fields.

### Maps and live tracking

-   Use the brand palette for routes and interface chrome, but preserve
    standard map conventions where they improve comprehension.
-   Make ambulance identity, last update time and tracking status
    visible.
-   Indicate stale or unavailable location data honestly.
-   Do not imply that a displayed location is live if it is delayed.
-   Provide a non-map status view for users who cannot interpret or
    access the map.

### Notifications

Notifications should state: - What happened. - Which referral or
transfer is affected. - Whether the recipient needs to act. - The time
of the event.

Avoid vague messages such as "Something changed." Use role-appropriate
detail and do not expose unnecessary patient information in SMS, push
notifications or email previews.

### Empty, loading and error states

Use calm, informative language: - Empty: "No referrals require your
attention." - Loading: "Loading referral information..." - Error: "We
couldn't load this referral. Try again or contact your administrator if
the issue continues." - Offline: "Connection lost. Updates will resume
when connectivity is restored."

Never make an empty state look like a system failure or make a failed
operation appear successful.

## 8. Iconography and illustration

### Icons

Use one consistent outline icon family throughout the product. Lucide is
a suitable starting point for the web application.

Guidelines: - Prefer simple, recognisable icons. - Use a consistent
stroke weight. - Pair unfamiliar icons with text labels. - Do not use
icons as the only indicator for critical actions. - Avoid mixing filled,
outlined and multicolour icon styles without a defined rule.

### Illustration

Use restrained, purposeful illustrations for onboarding, empty states
and marketing pages. Prefer abstract representations of connected
facilities, care pathways and coordinated teams.

Avoid: - Graphic depictions of medical emergencies. - Distressing
patient imagery. - Stock imagery that implies a real partnership or
endorsement. - Decorative medical symbols that could be confused with
official emergency-service marks.

### Photography

Where photography is used: - Show respectful, realistic healthcare
environments. - Represent healthcare workers and facilities
authentically. - Obtain appropriate image rights and consent. - Do not
use identifiable patient images without valid authorisation. - Do not
imply that a pictured institution is a Lifeline customer or partner
unless that is true.

## 9. Digital product application

### Web application

The web interface should feel like a professional operations
workspace: - Navy navigation and clear page hierarchy. - White and
subtle-neutral content surfaces. - Teal for primary actions and selected
states. - Clear tables, timelines and activity histories. - Strong
role-based navigation for referring facilities, receiving facilities,
ambulance teams and administrators.

### Ambulance mobile application

The mobile app should prioritise use while on duty: - Large, clearly
labelled touch targets. - High-contrast status and action controls. -
Minimal typing while moving. - Clear assignment details and destination
information. - Explicit collection, arrival and handover actions. -
Visible connectivity and location-permission states.

Do not encourage interaction while driving. Design workflows so that
updates can be made safely by an appropriate crew member when the
vehicle is stationary or by another authorised team member.

### Marketing website

The website should explain: 1. The referral and transfer coordination
problem. 2. How Lifeline connects facilities and ambulance teams. 3. How
the workflow operates. 4. Who the platform serves. 5. How security,
governance and interoperability are approached. 6. How a healthcare
network can request a demonstration or pilot discussion.

Do not publish unverified performance statistics, institutional logos,
partner claims or clinical outcome claims.

## 10. Accessibility, privacy and trust

The visual identity must support safe and responsible use in a
healthcare context.

-   Target WCAG 2.2 AA.
-   Provide keyboard access and visible focus states.
-   Use readable text sizes and sufficient contrast.
-   Never communicate clinical urgency through colour alone.
-   Avoid unnecessary patient details in notifications and shared
    screens.
-   Use role- and facility-scoped access.
-   Display timestamps and data freshness where they affect decisions.
-   Provide clear audit history for important actions.
-   Use neutral, non-judgemental language.
-   Ensure interface design does not imply that algorithmic
    prioritisation replaces clinician assessment.

Brand design cannot itself establish regulatory compliance. Privacy,
security, clinical safety, data governance and applicable Kenyan
health-sector requirements must be addressed through product
architecture, operational processes and formal review.

## 12. Brand governance

To keep the product consistent as more designers and engineers
contribute:

1.  Keep the approved logo files and colour tokens in a shared
    brand-assets directory.
2.  Maintain one source of truth for design tokens.
3.  Build shared components for buttons, inputs, status chips, cards,
    tables, alerts and timelines.
4.  Review new screens against these guidelines before release.
5.  Document any intentional exceptions to the colour, typography or
    component system.
6.  Do not create new status colours or typography styles casually.
7.  Review the brand system after usability testing with actual
    healthcare users.

## 13. Decisions to confirm before final brand approval

This document establishes a coherent proposed direction. The following
should be confirmed before treating it as the final corporate identity:

-   Whether the name **Lifeline** is legally and commercially available
    in the intended markets.
-   Domain name and social-handle availability.
-   Trademark and company-name clearance.
-   Final logo artwork and whether the proposed connected-path concept
    is approved.
-   Whether the product will use English only initially or support
    Kiswahili and other languages.
-   Whether the brand is intended for a single platform or a broader
    family of healthcare products.
-   Approval of the final colour palette through accessibility testing
    on actual screens.
-   Approval of the brand promise and tagline by the product owners and
    participating institutions.
