Introduction
Lifeline is a proposed digital healthcare coordination platform designed to improve the way hospitals, healthcare facilities and ambulance providers coordinate patient referrals and emergency transfers.
Kenya’s problem is coordination more than capacity. The Kenya Medical Association (KMA) says the country lacks not facilities but a properly functioning referral system. Critical care is scarce: only 25 public hospitals offer ICU, and 90% of Level 4 hospitals have no ICU or HDU. Referring teams still phone around for beds, specialists and equipment, then phone again for transport. Ambulance response can reach 60 minutes against a 15-20 minute target, and 34% of sampled ambulances were non-functional. 
The national 922 dispatch centre now covers scene-to-hospital emergencies, but inter-facility transfers remain managed by counties. Lifeline fills that gap. 
It connects referring facilities, receiving hospitals, ambulance providers, referral operations teams and network coordinators in one workflow. Teams can:
capture referrals and assess urgency;
find a receiving facility with the right beds, specialists and equipment;
get acceptance confirmed before the patient moves;
arrange transport, including through 922 and county ambulances;
track the transfer and confirm handover.
The long-term aim is referrals that are visible, traceable and measurable across Kenya’s healthcare networks.
Background and Context
Patient referrals are an essential component of healthcare delivery. A patient may require transfer when the referring facility cannot provide the necessary specialist services, diagnostic equipment, intensive care, surgical intervention, blood products or other medical support.
In such circumstances, the referring facility must coordinate several activities:
Establish the patient's clinical and transfer requirements.
Identify an appropriate receiving facility.
Confirm whether the receiving facility can accept the patient.
Identify appropriate transportation.
Coordinate ambulance dispatch and patient collection.
Monitor the patient's journey.
Confirm arrival and clinical handover.
Each activity involves different people, institutions and potentially different communication channels.
Without a connected coordination process, information can become fragmented, updates may be delayed, and responsibility for following up the transfer may become unclear.
This is particularly concerning when a patient's condition is deteriorating and time is critical.
Lifeline is proposed as a solution to improve the coordination of these activities.
Problem Definition
The Core Problem
Healthcare facilities in Kenya struggle to coordinate referrals and inter-facility transfers because information on receiving-facility capacity, transport availability and transfer progress sits across disconnected phone calls and manual processes. The Kenya Medical Association says the country lacks a properly functioning referral system, not facilities. Critical capacity is scarce and hard to locate: only 25 public hospitals offer ICU.
When a patient requires transfer, national guidelines require the referring clinician to call the receiving facility in advance to confirm the service is available, then arrange transport separately. Transport is itself unreliable: ambulance response runs up to 60 minutes against a 15-20 minute target, and 34% of sampled ambulances were non-functional. In practice, a Kenyatta National Hospital study found 67% of referring hospitals did not call ahead before sending a patient.
There is no consistently shared operational view of the referral journey across all parties. County dispatch centres lack real-time tracking and call-log systems, and the new national 922 centre covers scene-to-hospital emergencies, while inter-facility transfers remain managed by counties.
This creates several challenges:
Difficulty identifying an appropriate receiving facility
Referring facilities may not have immediate visibility into the availability of beds, specialists or required services at other hospitals.
Staff may have to contact several facilities individually.
Receiving facilities may be unable to accept patients despite initial expectations.
Information about facility capacity may become outdated before a decision is made.
Difficulty coordinating ambulance transportation
Ambulance availability may be unclear.
Facilities may not know which ambulance is closest or most appropriate.
Transport arrangements may be made independently of the referral process.
Delayed responses and unclear dispatch responsibilities may affect transfer timelines.
Ambulance costs and transport arrangements may lack consistent visibility.
Lack of transfer visibility
The referring facility may not know whether an ambulance has been assigned.
Receiving facilities may not know when the patient is expected.
Operations teams may struggle to establish the current status of a transfer.
Confirmation of patient arrival and handover may not be consistently recorded.
Fragmented communication and accountability
Critical updates may be spread across telephone calls, messaging applications and paper records.
Different stakeholders may have different versions of the same referral information.
There may be limited auditability of decisions and actions.
Management may lack reliable data to evaluate referral efficiency and transport performance.



Problem Statement
When patients require transfer between healthcare facilities, the coordination of receiving-facility acceptance, appropriate ambulance availability, dispatch and patient handover is often dependent on fragmented manual communication processes.
This limits visibility, creates coordination delays, increases administrative workload and makes it difficult to monitor the entire transfer journey.
There is a need for a connected, reliable and accountable referral coordination system that enables healthcare facilities and ambulance providers to coordinate patient transfers using shared, timely and actionable information.
Understanding the Problem Through the Referral Journey
To understand the problem properly, we should examine what happens when a patient requires transfer.
Stage 1 -  Patient Requires Referral
A patient is assessed at a referring facility. The attending clinical team determines that the patient requires services that cannot adequately be provided at the current facility.
The facility initiates a referral.
Potential challenges:-
Referral information is recorded manually.
Clinical information may need to be communicated repeatedly.
The urgency of the referral may not be consistently communicated.
There may be no shared digital referral record.
Stage 2 - Identify and Confirm the Receiving Facility
The referring facility identifies hospitals that may be capable of providing the required services.
The team contacts potential receiving facilities to establish:
Whether the hospital provides the required service.
Whether an appropriate bed is available.
Whether the required specialist or clinical team is available.
Whether the facility is currently able to accept the patient.
Whether any additional requirements must be met before acceptance.
Potential challenges:- 
Multiple telephone calls.
Delayed responses.
Incomplete or outdated capacity information.
Repeated referral attempts.
Uncertainty about whether acceptance has been confirmed.
Stage 3 -  Find Appropriate Transport
Once a receiving facility is identified, the referring facility arranges transportation.
The transport may need to support:
Basic patient transport.
Emergency medical transport.
Advanced life support.
Critical care transfers.
Specialised equipment or medical support.
Potential challenges:-
Ambulance availability is uncertain.
The appropriate ambulance may be located far away.
Transport providers may not have a shared dispatch workflow.
Cost and availability information may not be immediately accessible.
Stage 4 -  Dispatch and Patient Collection
An ambulance is assigned and dispatched to the referring facility.
Potential challenges:-
Unclear dispatch confirmation.
Delayed collection.
Limited visibility into ambulance movement.
Difficulty communicating changes to the receiving hospital.


Stage 5 -  Journey Monitoring
The patient is transported to the receiving facility.
Potential challenges:-
No shared view of journey progress.
The receiving facility may not know the patient's estimated arrival.
Changes in patient condition may require additional coordination.
Operations teams may need to make repeated calls for updates.
Stage 6 -  Patient Arrival and Handover
The ambulance arrives at the receiving facility and the patient is handed over to the receiving clinical team.
Potential challenges:
Handover confirmation may not be consistently documented.
Referral completion may not be immediately visible.
There may be no consolidated record of the entire transfer.
Proposed Solution Lifeline Intends to Offer
Lifeline is proposed as a healthcare referral and emergency patient-transfer coordination platform.
It will provide a common operational environment through which participating healthcare facilities and ambulance teams can coordinate referrals and monitor transfer progress.
Rather than replacing existing hospital information systems, Lifeline will operate as a coordination layer that connects the relevant participants and information involved in the referral process.
Core Solution Capabilities
Digital Referral Management
Enable referring facilities to initiate and manage patient referrals digitally.
Capabilities:
Capture patient identification and demographic information.
Capture clinical observations and relevant medical information.
Record referral reasons.
Attach relevant clinical documents.
Identify the referring clinician and facility.
Assign referral urgency.
Track referral status.
Maintain a referral history and audit trail.
Receiving Facility Coordination
Enable receiving hospitals to receive, review and respond to referral requests.
Capabilities:-
View incoming referral requests.
Review patient clinical information.
Indicate acceptance, rejection or a request for additional information.
Communicate acceptance conditions.
Indicate availability of relevant services and resources.
Provide estimated readiness or receiving information.
Maintain referral response records.
Facility capacity information should be updated and validated by authorised hospital personnel or integrated hospital systems.
Clinical Priority Support
Support healthcare teams in communicating and assessing patient urgency.
The proposed use of Modified Early Warning Score (MEWS) and relevant clinical indicators could help standardise the presentation of patient observations and support urgency assessment.
Potential capabilities:
Record vital signs.
Calculate an appropriate clinical early-warning score.
Display urgency indicators.
Highlight abnormal observations.
Support escalation workflows.
Maintain clinical assessment history.
Note: MEWS should support, not replace, clinical judgement. Its implementation requires clinical governance, validation, agreed escalation thresholds and appropriate clinical oversight.
Ambulance Coordination and Dispatch
Enable healthcare facilities and operations teams to identify and coordinate available transport.
Capabilities:-
Ambulance registration.
Ambulance availability status.
Ambulance location.
Transport capability classification.
Equipment and crew information.
Dispatch requests.
Assignment and acceptance.
Dispatch status updates.
Estimated arrival information.
Trip and transport records.
Lifeline intends to  match transport based on clinical requirements, ambulance capability, availability, location and operational constraints rather than distance alone.

Capabilities:-

Ambulance assignment notifications.
Dispatch notifications.
Journey progress updates.
Delayed arrival alerts.

Provide operational intelligence for hospitals, ambulance providers and healthcare administrators.
Potential reporting areas:

Referral acceptance and rejection rates.

Time taken to assign an ambulance.

Primary responsibilities:-
Initiate referrals.
Capture patient information.
Provide clinical observations.
Specify transfer requirements.
Monitor acceptance.
Request transport.
Confirm patient collection.
Confirm handover.
Receiving Hospital
The hospital expected to receive the patient.
Primary responsibilities: - 
Receive referral requests.
Review clinical information.
Assess receiving capacity.
Accept or decline referrals.
Communicate receiving requirements.
Prepare for patient arrival.
Confirm patient receipt.

 Ambulance Team / Transport Provider
The ambulance service responsible for patient transportation.
Primary responsibilities:-
Register available vehicles.
Maintain ambulance status.
Declare transport capabilities.
Receive dispatch requests.
Accept or decline assignments.
Update dispatch and collection status.
Share journey progress.
Confirm arrival and transport completion.

The operational coordination team responsible for overseeing participating facilities and transport services.
Primary responsibilities:-
Monitor active referrals.
Monitor transport availability.
Support coordination and escalation.
Monitor delayed transfers.
Manage participating institutions.
Generate operational reports.
Review service performance.






Proposed Workflow
The proposed Lifeline workflow can be summarised into four primary stages.
Stage
Process
Expected outcome
1. Capture
Record patient information, clinical observations and referral reason.
Complete digital referral
2. Prioritise
Present clinical indicators and urgency assessment.
Clearer urgency communication
3. Match & Dispatch
Coordinate receiving facility acceptance and appropriate available transport.
Confirmed destination and transport
4. Track
Monitor dispatch, collection, journey and handover.
Traceable completed transfer

The workflow should preserve the distinction between clinical decisions and operational coordination.
A receiving facility's acceptance must be explicitly confirmed by authorised personnel. An ambulance assignment must also be confirmed by the responsible transport provider.

An important strategic consideration is that Lifeline should not position itself as a replacement for existing emergency response channels, hospital information systems or ambulance management solutions.
Instead, it should connect the processes that currently operate independently.
Existing Healthcare Infrastructure
The existing environment  include:
Emergency access and response channels, including 922 where applicable.
Hospital Information Systems (HIS).
Electronic Medical Records (EMR).
Manual referral processes.
Telephone and messaging communication.
Standalone ambulance fleet management tools.
Existing ambulance providers and dispatch operations.
These systems and services serve different purposes.

Evidence and Research Foundation
The original pitch references the Kenya Ministry of Health's Confidential Enquiry into Maternal Deaths: Summary of Findings (2017).
The intended argument is that referral pathways are an important patient-safety concern.
The pitch also references maternal deaths reviewed involving women who had been referred from another facility.
This is potentially useful evidence for establishing the importance of referral coordination. However, the actual report, relevant page, statistic and wording should be verified before the information is used in an investor presentation or formal product documentation.
An important distinction must be maintained:
The statistic concerns deaths reviewed in the enquiry.
It should not be presented as representing all referrals or all maternal deaths nationally.
It does not independently establish that a particular percentage of deaths was caused by poor referral coordination.
Additional research should establish the current scale of referral coordination challenges in Kenya, including ambulance availability, referral delays, hospital capacity visibility and existing referral management practices.
Key Risks and Considerations
Lifeline operates in a sensitive healthcare environment. Its development ccounts for more than software functionality.
Data Protection and Patient Privacy
The platform will handle sensitive personal and health information.
It must address:
Compliance with Kenya's Data Protection Act, 2019.
Appropriate handling of sensitive personal data.
Role-based access control.
Secure storage and transmission.
Audit trails.
Data retention and deletion policies.
Data-sharing agreements between participating institutions.
Appropriate privacy notices and consent or other lawful processing grounds.
Clinical Safety
Clinical priority indicators must be appropriately validated.
Lifeline must not automatically make clinical decisions that should remain with qualified healthcare professionals.
Facility Capacity Accuracy
A receiving hospital's capacity information must be reliable and current.
Incorrect bed or service availability information could result in inappropriate referral decisions.
Ambulance Availability Accuracy
Ambulance status must be updated consistently by providers.
A vehicle shown as available but actually unavailable could create additional delays.
Connectivity and Infrastructure
The platform must accommodate facilities with varying network conditions.
Mobile-first workflows, offline resilience where appropriate and reliable synchronisation should be considered.
Institutional Adoption
Successful implementation depends on the participation of hospitals, healthcare administrators and ambulance providers.
The platform must fit into existing operational procedures rather than introduce unnecessary administrative work.
Integration and Interoperability
The system should be designed to integrate with existing healthcare systems without requiring participating institutions to replace their entire technology infrastructure.
